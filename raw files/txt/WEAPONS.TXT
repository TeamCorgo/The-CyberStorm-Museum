=====================================================================
LEGEND
=====================================================================

Required rank: The rank that the weapon first becomes available.

Armor factor:  Armor factor.   This is a factor that says how much damage 
               applied to the component is passed through.  An Armor factor
               of 40 means that only 40% of the damage is applied to the
               component, and that 60% is deflected by the components
               toughness.  An armor factor of 200 means that the damage is
               effectively doubled, meaning the component is not very 
               sturdy.

Weight:        The effective weight of the weapon.

Base Cost:     The original cost of the weapon at the rank level at 
               which the weapon first becomes available.  Note that the
               longer a weapon has been available, the cheaper it is to
               produce.

Repair factor ( % of Base Cost ): 
               How much does the weapon cost to repair vs. new.
               A factor of 100 would mean it costs the same (100%)
               to repair a component that was destroyed as buying a 
               new one.  A factor of 50 would mean that it costs
               only half as much (50%) to repair the component as buying
               a new one.

Ammo Capacity: The ammunition capacity of the weapon.

Mount restrictions: Shows which HERC models can carry this weapon.

Max shots per turn: Number of times the weapon may fire each turn               

Max targetable range:  Maximum range of the weapon in hexes.

Range increment: Determines the ability of the weapon to hit 
   accurately at a distance.  Out to a distance in hexes equal to 
   the range increment, the chance to hit is 100%.  Then, between 
   that distance and twice that distance, the chance of a hit is 80%.
   This continues, reducing by 20% for each subsequent range
   increment. Weapons without range increments always have a 100%
   base chance of a hit.         
              
Damage increment(DI): Determines the ability of the weapon to 
   damage at a distance.  Out to a distance in hexes equal to the
   DI, the damage inflicted is full.  Then, between that distance
   and twice that distance, the amount of damage inflicted is half
   of the original damage amount.  This continues, reducing by half
   of the current damage amount for each subsequent damage increment.
   
Indirect Fire: Does the weapon the ability to fire indirectly?

Skill used: The bioderm skill rating used to determine the
   probability of hitting a target.

Damaged weapon effects: The way a weapon responds to being damaged.

Disable percent: The damage percentage at which the weapon becomes
   disabled and inoperable.

Energy cost per use: The number of energy points required to fire
   the weapon once.

Damage vs. shields: The amount of damage each hit inflicts against
   the target's shield.
   IMPORTANT NOTE: To get the full damage for the weapon, multiply
                   by the 'Number of hits/shot'

Damage vs. no shields: The amount of damage each hit inflicts
   against the target's armor.           
   IMPORTANT NOTE: To get the full damage for the weapon, multiply
                   by the 'Number of hits/shot'

Damage that penetrates shields: The amount of damage each hit inflicts
   against a target's armor if that target is shielded.
   IMPORTANT NOTE: To get the full damage for the weapon, multiply
                   by the 'Number of hits/shot'

Damage pct carryover:  The percentage of remaining shield damage
   carried over from shields to armor if the shields buckle with a hit.

Number of hits/shot: The number of hits each shot inflicts. Each 
   firing of a weapon is a single "shot", but can have several "hits".
   If a weapon is listed as having 10 hits per shot and each hit
   does 25 points of damage, it will do a total of 250 points of damage.

Damage area (hexes): The number of hexes each shot damages.

at target hex:  The damage percentage inflicted on the target 
   hex.(Always 100%)

at any hex distance 1: The percentage of damage inflicted on targets
   one hex away from the target hex.

at any hex distance 2: The percentage of damage inflicted on targets
   two hexes away from the target hex.

at any hex distance 3: The percentage of damage inflicted on targets
   three hexes away from the target hex.  

Apply damage to hex even in miss:  (Yes/No)
   This defines whether the weapon will has the possibility of doing any
   damage to target that it missed.  A laser is pinpoint enough that when
   it misses, it truly misses.  Most missiles have a large enough explosion
   that if they miss by a little but still hit their general target hex, 
   they will do their damage.
